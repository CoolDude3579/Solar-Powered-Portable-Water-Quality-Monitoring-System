# LTspice power-supply simulations

| File | Circuit | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| `solar_charging_24h.asc` | 6 V / 2 W solar panel → TP4056 charger → 2000 mAh 18650 → device load | **1 second of simulation = 1 hour.** t = 0 is midnight and t = 12 is noon. Plot `V(bat)`, `V(pvo)`, `I(Vichg)` (charge current) and `I(Vild)` (load current). The energy totals in Wh are under *View → SPICE Error Log*. |
| `boost_converter_5v.asc` | 3.7 V battery → 5.0 V boost (500 kHz, PI control, soft-start) with a +250 mA load step at 3.5 ms | Plot `V(out)`, `I(L1)`. Add `.step param Vin list 3.3 3.7 4.2` to see regulation across the battery range. |

**Open:** double-click the `.asc` file, or use LTspice → *File → Open*. Then click **Run** (the running-man icon) and click a net label to plot it.

## How the models work

- **Solar panel:** single-diode model, with photocurrent `0.40 A × irradiance`, diode `Is = 1 nA, N = 14` (giving about 7.2 V open-circuit), and series/shunt resistors. The irradiance follows a clear-day curve that peaks at 1000 W/m² at noon.
- **TP4056:** a behavioural current source that reproduces the chip's charging behaviour:
  - constant current up to 1 A, limited by what the panel can supply
  - constant voltage at 4.2 V (current tapers off as the battery fills)
  - stops charging when the input falls below about 4 V
  - no reverse current back into the panel
- **Battery:** a capacitor in series with 0.08 Ω. A 2000 mAh cell holds 7200 C between 3.0 and 4.2 V, which is about 6000 F. That is divided by 3600 so hours run as seconds.
- **Load:** 1 W for 30 s every 5 min (measure + WiFi upload), 10 mW deep sleep otherwise, drawn through the boost converter at 85 % efficiency.
- **Boost converter:** 10 µH inductor, SS14 Schottky diode, 220 µF electrolytic output capacitor (its ESR damps the LC resonance), and an ideal MOSFET switch. A sawtooth PWM comparator is driven by an integral controller with anti-windup and a 1 ms soft-start.

## Verified results (ngspice, same netlist: `python3 generate.py --check`, `python3 plot_results.py`)

- Harvested **6.4 Wh**, consumed **3.1 Wh**; battery went from 3.55 V at midnight to the 4.2 V limit by about 13:00 and was at 4.06 V the next midnight.
- Boost output **5.00 V** at battery 4.2 / 3.7 / 3.3 V, with about 100 mV peak-to-peak ripple. It dips to about 4.85 V on the load step and recovers in about 0.5 ms.

The `.asc` schematics and `.cir` netlists are both generated by `generate.py` from one description, so they cannot drift apart. Edit `generate.py`, then re-run it.
